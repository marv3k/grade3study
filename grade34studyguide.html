Unit 1: Computer Parts (Hardware)

All the parts of a computer you can see and touch

Inside the Computer

The Computer's Brain (CPU)

The little chip inside that thinks, does math, and tells all the other parts what to do.

Working Memory (RAM)

Short-term memory used while you are playing or typing right now. When you turn the computer off, this memory wipes clean!

Saving Space (Hard Drive)

Long-term memory—just like a backpack or toy box—that keeps your stories, pictures, and games safe even when the computer is turned off.

Input vs. Output Parts

➡️ Input Parts

Parts that send your words, clicks, or voice INTO the computer:

  • Keyboard (types letters)
  • Mouse / Trackpad (clicks)
  • Microphone (records voice)
  • Webcam (takes video)
⬅️ Output Parts

Parts that bring pictures, sound, or paper OUT of the computer to you:

  • Monitor / Screen (shows pictures)
  • Printer (prints onto paper)
  • Speakers (plays sound)
  • Headphones (just for your ears)

Unit 2: Staying Safe & Kind Online

How to stay safe, protect your secrets, and be a good friend on the internet

🔒 Private Facts About You (PII)

Private facts that tell strangers who you are or where you live. Never share these online!

Keep Secret: Your full name, home address, phone number, school name, birthday, and passwords.

🔑 Super-Strong Secret Passwords

A strong password works like a sturdy lock on your bedroom door. Make it long and mix words, numbers, and symbols.

Easy to Guess: 123456 or password
Super Strong: Green$Taco#2026

👣 Your Online Trail (Digital Footprint)

The trail of clues you leave behind when you go online—like websites you visit, videos you watch, and photos or comments you share. Always think before you post because your online trail can stay there forever!

🛡️ Being Kind Online: Upstander vs. Bystander

Cyberbullying is using a computer, tablet, or phone to be mean to someone on purpose.

  • • Bystander: Watches mean things happen online and does nothing.
  • • Upstander: Stands up to help! Says kind words to the person being hurt and tells a trusted adult right away.

Unit 3: Typing, Shortcuts & Naming Files

Typing neatly, saving time with key tricks, and naming your saved work

Home Row Keys

The home base where your fingers rest: A S D F (left hand) and J K L ; (right hand). The F and J keys have tiny bumps so your pointer fingers can find them without looking!

Shift vs. Caps Lock

Hold Shift to type just ONE capital letter or a top symbol (like ! or ?). Only tap Caps Lock when typing an entire word or title in ALL CAPITAL LETTERS.

Spaces & Smart File Names

Spacing Rule: Put one space AFTER a period or comma, never before.

File Naming: Use clear names with no spaces, like Topic_Name_Date (Example: Tables_Maya_Nov16).

Unit 4: Page Setup & How Your Page Looks

Choosing tall or wide paper, white edge borders, headers, and footers

Page Direction (Orientation)

📄 Tall Page (Portrait)

Standing up tall (taller than it is wide). Best for stories, letters, and reports.

🖼️ Wide Page (Landscape)

Turned sideways (wider than it is tall). Best for signs, posters, and awards.

White Edge Borders (Margins)

The blank white space around the edges of a page so your words don't get cut off when you print. Normal Margins leave 1 inch of white space; Narrow Margins leave half an inch (0.5 in) so big tables fit better.

Header vs. Footer

Think of your own body: your head is at the top, and your feet are at the bottom!

HEADER (Top Edge): Name & Date Shows on every page
Main Page Area (Where your story or table goes)
FOOTER (Bottom Edge) Page 1 (Page Numbers)

Unit 5: Lists, Tables & Pictures

Making your work look neat, organized, and fun to read

Lining Up Your Words (Alignment)

  • • Left Side: Used for normal stories and paragraphs.
  • • Middle (Center): Used for big titles at the top of a page.
  • • Right Side: Often used for the date at the top of a page.

Bullet Points vs. Numbered Lists

  • • Bullet Points (•): Use when order does NOT matter (like a camping packing list or favorite foods).
  • • Numbered List (1, 2, 3): Use when order DOES matter (like recipe steps or your Top 3 favorite games).

Line Spacing & Indenting

  • • Double Spacing (2.0): Leaves extra breathing room between lines of words so your teacher can read easily.
  • • Indent Rule: Press the Tab key ONCE at the start of a paragraph (never press Spacebar 5 times!).

Tables (A Neat Grid of Boxes)

A table sorts facts into neat boxes (just like a class schedule):

  • • Columns: Go straight up and down (like tall pillars).
  • • Rows: Go flat side to side (like rows of seats).
  • • Cells: Each little box where you type your words.
  • • Table Tip: Press Tab to jump smoothly to the next box!

Pictures & Wrap Text

  • • Corner Dots Rule: Always drag one of the 4 corner dots (handles) when making a picture bigger or smaller so it doesn't look stretched or squished!
  • • Wrap Text: Lets your words wrap neatly around a picture just like a storybook or magazine.
