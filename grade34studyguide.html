Unit 1: Computer Hardware & Parts

The physical parts of a computer you can see and touch

Inside the Computer

CPU (Central Processing Unit)

The "brain" of the computer. It follows instructions, does math, and tells all the other parts what to do.

RAM (Working Memory)

Short-term memory used while you are working on apps right now. Everything in RAM clears out when you turn the computer off.

Storage (Hard Drive / SSD)

Long-term memory (like a digital backpack). It keeps your documents, photos, and games safely saved even when the power is turned off.

Input vs. Output Devices

➡️ Input Devices

Tools that send information INTO the computer:

  • Keyboard (types letters)
  • Mouse / Trackpad (clicks)
  • Microphone (records voice)
  • Webcam (captures video)
⬅️ Output Devices

Tools that send information OUT of the computer to you:

  • Monitor / Screen (shows pictures)
  • Printer (prints onto paper)
  • Speakers (plays sound)
  • Headphones (private sound)

Unit 2: Digital Citizenship & Online Safety

Staying safe, private, and kind whenever we use the internet

🔒 PII (Personal Information)

Private details that tell strangers who you are or where to find you. Never share PII online!

Keep Private: Full name, home address, phone number, school name, birthday, and passwords.

🔑 Strong Passwords (Passphrases)

A strong password acts like a sturdy lock on your front door. Make it long and mix words, numbers, and symbols.

Weak: 123456 or password
Strong: Green$Taco#2026

👣 Digital Footprint

The trail of clues you leave behind when you go online—like websites you visit, videos you watch, and photos or comments you post. Think before you post because a digital footprint can stay online forever!

🛡️ Cyberbullying: Upstander vs. Bystander

Cyberbullying is using a computer, tablet, or phone to be mean to someone on purpose.

  • • Bystander: Sees mean behavior happen online and does nothing.
  • • Upstander: Stands up to help! Offers kindness to the person being hurt and tells a trusted adult right away.

Unit 3: Keyboarding, Shortcuts & File Naming

Typing accurately, saving time with shortcuts, and organizing files

Home Row Keys

Where your fingers rest when typing: A S D F (left hand) and J K L ; (right hand). The F and J keys have tiny raised bumps so your pointer fingers can find them without looking!

Shift vs. Caps Lock

Hold Shift to type just ONE capital letter or a top symbol (like ! or ?). Only tap Caps Lock when typing an entire word or title in ALL CAPITAL LETTERS.

Punctuation & File Names

Spacing Rule: Put one space AFTER a comma or period, never before.

File Naming: Use clear names with no spaces, like Topic_Name_Date (Example: Tables_Maya_Nov16).

Unit 4: Page Setup & Document Layout

Setting up your paper direction, borders, headers, and footers

Page Orientation (Which Way the Paper Turns)

📄 Portrait (Tall)

Vertical setup (taller than it is wide). Best for stories, letters, and reports.

🖼️ Landscape (Wide)

Horizontal setup (wider than it is tall). Best for signs, certificates, and wide charts.

Margins (The White Safety Border)

The blank white border around the edges of a page so words do not get cut off when printed. Normal Margins = 1 inch on all sides; Narrow Margins = 0.5 inches (gives extra room for big tables).

Header vs. Footer

Think of your own body: your head is at the top, and your feet are at the bottom!

HEADER (Top Margin): Name & Date Repeats on every page
Main Document Body (Where your story or table goes)
FOOTER (Bottom Margin) Page 1 (Automatic Page Numbers)

Unit 5: Organizing Text, Lists, Tables & Images

Making documents look neat, organized, and easy to read

Text Alignment

  • • Left-Aligned: Used for normal paragraphs and stories.
  • • Centered: Used for main titles at the top of a page.
  • • Right-Aligned: Often used for the date in a header.

Bulleted Lists vs. Numbered Lists

  • • Bulleted List (•): Use when order does NOT matter (example: a camping packing list or favorite foods).
  • • Numbered List (1, 2, 3): Use when order DOES matter (example: recipe steps or Top 3 rankings).

Line Spacing & Paragraph Indents

  • • Double Spacing (2.0): Adds extra space between lines so teachers can read easily and write notes.
  • • Indent Rule: Press the Tab key ONCE at the beginning of a paragraph (never press Spacebar 5 times!).

Tables (Grids Made of Boxes)

A table organizes facts into neat boxes (like a class schedule):

  • • Columns: Go straight up and down (vertical).
  • • Rows: Go flat side to side (horizontal).
  • • Cells: Each single box where a column and row meet.
  • • Table Tip: Press Tab to jump smoothly to the next cell!

Images & Text Wrapping

  • • Corner Handles Rule: Always drag one of the 4 corner handles when resizing a picture so it doesn't look stretched or squished!
  • • Wrap Text: Lets your sentences flow neatly around the side of a picture like a magazine page.
